🤝Informed consent

Users agree knowingly and freely. Consent is separate for each use of data, written in plain language, and never pre-ticked.

Comes up in Decision 1
🎯Data minimisation

Collect only what the feature needs, keep it only as long as needed, and protect it. Data you never collect cannot leak.

Comes up in Decision 2
🔐Privacy by default

The safest settings are on from the start. Users can open things up, but they should not have to dig to protect themselves.

Comes up in Decision 6
🎭No dark patterns

Interfaces must not trick people with hidden buttons, guilt messages or confusing wording to make them act against their interests.

Comes up in Decision 3
🧒Child safety

Children need extra protection: age checks, parental consent and minimal data. Laws such as COPPA (USA) and the UK GDPR set rules for this.

Comes up in Decision 4
📣Transparency and accountability

Tell users quickly when something goes wrong. Data protection laws such as the GDPR and Jamaica’s Data Protection Act 2020 expect breaches to be handled openly.

Comes up in Decision 5
🔔Digital wellbeing

Design for people’s time and attention, not against it. Give users control over notifications, avoid guilt-based reminders, and make it easy to take a break.

Comes up in Decision 7
♿Accessibility

Everyone should be able to use your product, including people who are blind, have low vision, limited mobility or hearing loss. Build in screen-reader support, scalable text, good colour contrast and large tap targets from the start.

Comes up in Decision 8
🗑️Right to deletion

People should be able to delete their account and personal data without a fight. Make the process clear and simple, and actually delete the data instead of just hiding it. Laws such as the GDPR give people a right to erasure in many cases.

Comes up in Decision 9
⚖️Fairness in AI

Test automated features on many kinds of people before launch. A model trained on data that leaves some groups out can work worse for them. Fix known bias, or be honest about its limits.

Comes up in Decision 10
⭐Honest marketing

Ratings, reviews and testimonials must reflect real experiences. Fake or paid-for reviews deceive the public, and many countries have consumer protection rules against them.

Comes up in Decision 11
📦Data stewardship

Data collected under one set of promises should not pass to someone else without telling users and giving them a real choice. Data protection laws expect transfers, such as the sale of a company, to be handled openly.
